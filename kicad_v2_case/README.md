# Haven v2 BTE charging case: schematic

Generated by `tools/gen_v2_case_schematic.py` (edit the script, not the `.kicad_sch`).
Charge-only, no microcontroller. 26 parts, 11 nets. `kicad-cli sch erc`: 0 violations.

```
USB-C (5 V sink: CC1/CC2 5.1k) ── MCP73831 charger, 250 mA ── ~800 mAh 1S LiPo (with PCM), JST-SH
                                       └─ red LED: on while charging
LiPo ── TPS61023 boost, 4.95 V, always on ── pogo pins: L pod (VBUS, GND), R pod (VBUS, GND)
```

## Decisions
- **No IP5306.** It switches its 5 V output off below ~45 mA, and two charging pods (~40 mA
  each, falling as they fill) sit right at that edge.
- **No BQ25619.** Its 5 V boost needs an I2C write to enable, so it needs a microcontroller.
- **Charger:** MCP73831 (LCSC C424093). The TP4054 has the same pinout and the same
  ~1000/R(kΩ) mA rule, so it is a drop-in if MCP73831 stock is short. R3 = 4.02k gives ~250 mA.
- **Boost:** TI TPS61023 (LCSC C1852149), values from its datasheet (SLVSF14B, Figure 8-1):
  1 µH, 10 µF in, 2 × 22 µF out, 732k/100k → 4.95 V. EN is tied to VIN, so it is always on:
  20–30 µA standby, about 3 years on a full 800 mAh cell. A short on the pogo pins folds the
  output back to ~350 mA, and it restarts when the short is released (datasheet 7.3.7).
- **Per-pod current:** each pod's nPM1100 limits itself to 100 mA, so no per-pod switch.
- **Battery:** 800 mAh is about 3 full recharges of both pods (2 × ~100 mAh per recharge,
  ~85% boost efficiency, plus margin). Buy a cell **with** a protection board (PCM); the board has
  no undervoltage cut-off of its own.
- **Pogo pins:** 2 per pod, **5.0 mm apart**, centred on the pod's 3 mm magnet. They mate the two
  Ø1.5 mm contacts in the pod shell's bottom end (haven-hardware `v2_bte`, PAD_PITCH 2.5 mm), not
  the board pads directly: on the first build each shell contact is a short wire to P1/P2.
  The footprint is a 2.2 mm round seat for a 2.0 mm-base SMD spring pin; the actual part is open.
- ESD (TPD1E05U06, same as the pod) on USB VBUS and on the pogo VBUS rail.

## Layout (2026-11-05)
- 44 × 15.5 mm, 2 layers, 1.0 mm, all parts on top. Outline and pin positions come from
  haven-hardware `mechanical_cad/v2_bte/case.json` (the board sits on the case's bottom plate
  under the pods).
- `kicad-cli pcb drc --schematic-parity --severity-all`: **0 violations, 0 unconnected, 0 parity
  issues.** JLCPCB standard 2-layer rules (0.15 mm clearance, 0.6/0.3 mm vias).
- Flow: `tools/gen_v2_case_schematic.py` → `tools/gen_v2_case_pcb.py` (placement, the SW trace,
  pours) → `tools/route_v2_case_pcb.py` (Freerouting) → `tools/stitch_v2_case_pcb.py` (GND
  stitching). One GND island (under R2) got a via by hand after stitching.
- The spring pins sit exactly under the pod contacts. USB-C (J1) is at x = −2 mm with its mouth
  flush with the back edge, and the charge LED (D2) is at the back edge behind the case's light hole.
- Boost layout: U2's SW pin sits straight under L1's SW pad (0.35 mm trace, ~1.3 mm long). The
  output caps are on the side the VOUT pin faces, and the input cap is beside L1's VBAT pad.
- Spring pins: candidate Kinghelm KH-PG2011005-DZ (LCSC C2842935), SMD, 11 mm free, 2 mm base, on
  the 2.2 mm round seat. The case puts the board top 10.0 mm below the pod contacts (~1 mm of
  compression); the stroke isn't published, so measure a real pin and adjust `POGO_WORK_H` in
  haven-hardware `case.py`.

## Firmware impact
- Pods see 5 V on VBUS whenever they sit in the case (VBUS_DET). The pod firmware should drop to
  its lowest-power state there (no advertising), or it will drain the case battery.
