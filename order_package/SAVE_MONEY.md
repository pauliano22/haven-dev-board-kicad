# Cutting the ~$500 order cost (researched 2026-09-18; verify current terms)

## 1. PCBWay student sponsorship (do this BEFORE ordering)
- 10-15% off future orders, and reportedly "big discounts" on assembly, for
  students. You register with your **.edu email** and post your project
  (school name + what it is). They email a coupon, reportedly within 24 h.
  Contact: sponsor@PCBWay.com. You still pay shipping.
- Coupons often can't be combined or applied after checkout - get the code
  first, then place the order.

## 2. Free ADAU1860 samples from Analog Devices (the expensive chip)
- ADI gives free samples: typically 2 pieces per part, up to 4 parts per
  request per 14 days; requires registering with a **university/company
  email** (no gmail). Some parts are excluded (e.g. full reel only) - use the
  sample site's availability lookup for ADAU1860. Higher limits: ask
  SampleSupport@analog.com.
- Why it matters: the PCBA house will probably want you to supply this
  BGA (consignment). Sampled chips could cover 2 boards' worth for free.
  Ask the PCBWay/JLC rep whether they'll place customer-supplied BGAs and
  whether there's a fee.
- Same idea for other TI/ADI parts on the BOM (samples from ti.com). Only for
  parts the assembler can't stock; samples are for evaluation/prototypes.

## 3. Order fewer boards? Probably not worth it
Earlier research found assembly setup cost is flat at low quantity (qty 5 ~
qty 20), so 2 boards isn't much cheaper than 5, and 5 protects against a
bad first assembly. Keep 5 unless the quote clearly says otherwise.

## 4. Other levers (smaller)
- JLCPCB has coupon center offers for new customers; compare final PCBWay vs
  JLC quotes after discounts, not before.
- Standard shipping over express.
- The funding routes in haven-workspace/FUNDING_RESEARCH.md are slower
  (weeks-months) - not for this first order.
